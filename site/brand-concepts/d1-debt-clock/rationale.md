# D1 — Debt clock

The National Debt Clock billboard idiom, used literally: a black LED board in a dark housing with corner bolts, a white
condensed marquee ($DEFICIT, Oswald 700), and the debt itself in red seven-segment digits. The digits are not a font: each
glyph is seven hexagonal segments built from boxes, the unlit segments left faintly visible as they are on a real board, and
the lit ones carry a fine LED-dot matrix instead of a flat fill. The dollar sign is a seven-segment S with a bar through it,
which is how such boards actually fake it. The avatar reduces the whole coin to one figure — $1b, the unit of debt per burn —
so at 48 px it is three red glyphs on black, unmistakably a counter. Amber is reserved for what we burned; red for what they
borrowed. Strap: BURNS WHEN AMERICA BORROWS. Why it does not read as generated: a specific, dated public object (the clock),
component-level construction with its real imperfections (ghost segments, dot pitch, a faked $), and no gradients, glow or
rounded cards — the only shading is the housing.
