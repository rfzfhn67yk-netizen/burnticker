1. Abstract

Coins that burn on real-world results — with receipts. Burnticker is a launchpad for Solana memecoins whose supply burns when a settled real-world result says so. The receipt is the product. Every burn leaves a receipt that a stranger can recompute. On Burnticker, nothing is ever won — things are only burned.

A creator ties each coin to a rule. The rule picks events from a resolution source, which is called the selector, and says how much burns for each outcome, which is called the mapping. The source is either a primary source with an independent cross-read, or the on-chain payout of an adjudicated Polymarket market. The rule is hashed and locked when the coin is created. When an event that the selector picks settles, attesters sign the settled reading. The settlement program (burn_receipts) then spends a share of the coin's own creator-fee pool buying the coin on its Raydium CPMM pool. It burns every token it receives. The same instruction writes a memo with the reading, the arithmetic and the rule hash. Anyone can recompute that memo from the public source and the published rule.

The platform has two layers. The first is the engine, which is the launchpad and the program. Rules are locked at launch, readings are signed before use, and a program runs the burns instead of a person promising them. The second layer is the show, and it is built from the same receipts. It has team coins scored by burns, league by league. It has rivalry pairs (section 5), whose pages keep the tally. It has Burn Derby pairs and brackets (section 5.2). A season is a scoreboard of burns. There is one per league and season, and there is never a ranking across leagues. When a season ends, the coin with the most receipts is its league's champion. The champion is a stat, never a recipient. No coin pays anything on any outcome.

Burnticker is run by one independent operator, reachable at contact@burnticker.com. The operator’s identity is held privately until a legal entity is formed. Burnticker is not an investment product. A coin holds no position in any market, and the rule says what the supply does, not what the price does. The program is not audited by a security firm.

2. The problem with "burn" claims today

Most token burns are statements by whoever controls the tokens. Three patterns show up again and again.

  • Operator-controlled burns. A team wallet decides when to burn and how much. The same wallet can also sell.
  • Unverifiable triggers. "We burn when X happens" names an event, but it names no source, unit or formula. Nobody can check that a burn matches the event, or that a burn was skipped.
  • Team allocations. Burning tokens that a team got for free removes supply the market never held. The allocation also gives insiders a position that the burn posts serve.

Burnticker takes the trigger, the amount and the custody away from the operator, and it leaves a receipt that a stranger can recompute. The rule is fixed before the first trade. The reading comes from an adjudicated source and is signed by named keys. The money sits in an account that has no withdraw instruction. The program computes the amount and writes the receipt in the same instruction as the burn.

3. Design

3.1 The rule: a selector and an outcome–burn mapping

Locking. lockRule writes the validated rule as canonical JSON, with keys sorted, a two-space indent and a trailing newline. It hashes those bytes with sha256. The hash goes into three places: TokenConfig.rules_sha256 at initialize_token, the footer of the token description, and every on-chain memo as rules=sha256:<first 12 hex>. No instruction changes it. A different rule makes a different coin.

Finality. A Polymarket market counts once its payout vector is on chain. A primary source's period counts once the period is complete (src/lib/burnticker/sources/attestedHttp.ts). Some coins were launched on a Kalshi rule before 2026-10-10. For those coins, a Kalshi market counts when its status is finalized, or when it is settled and settlement_ts + settlement_timer_seconds has passed (src/lib/burnticker/oracle/kalshi.ts). The rule reads only settled results, never prices. The token page shows a "market-implied chance of the next burn", and that figure is labelled display-only.

3.2 Sources

Coins settle on primary sources. Prediction markets serve for discovery, and as optional cross-checks where their terms permit. On 2026-10-10 the owner decided that Kalshi is not a settlement input. Kalshi's Developer Agreement limits API use to a member's own trading. Its Data Terms forbid using Kalshi data to settle products, and they forbid caching or redistributing it. A coin settles on one of two things. The first is a primary source read together with an independent cross-read of the same fact, from another operator on another domain. The second is the payout vector of an adjudicated Polymarket market, as Polygon's Conditional Tokens contract stores it (section 3.4). The Kalshi adapter (src/lib/burnticker/oracle/kalshi.ts) remains for discovery behind BURNTICKER_KALSHI_ENABLED. The creation preflight refuses every Kalshi rule.

Polymarket binary markets are the adjudicated source. This has been the case since 2026-10-10. Gamma (https://gamma-api.polymarket.com) finds the markets. The result is the payout vector that the Conditional Tokens contract on Polygon stores for the market's condition id, read with eth_call. A reading counts only once that payout is on chain, and a 50-50 payout is a void (src/lib/burnticker/oracle/polymarket.ts). A single game pairs on Polymarket's moneyline market. Team coins have no settlement route on Polymarket, because it lists the two teams in an order that changes from game to game.

First-party feeds are the primary path. The source list (config/burnticker/sources.json) holds 34 sources, four of them cross-read-only. One generic adapter reads them all (src/lib/burnticker/sources/attestedHttp.ts). They include Treasury, BLS, NOAA, Launch Library 2, JPL, Wikimedia and others. Each source has a saved fixture, plausibility bounds, a fixed unit, a direction, a baseline and a carry. Each one also names the operator that runs the endpoint. Where another operator publishes the same number, the two sources share a fact. Sources that need a key are present but not launchable.

$DEFICIT is the stated exception. The operator's own first coin reads U.S. Treasury "Debt to the Penny" (tot_pub_debt_out_amt) directly. Every full $1,000,000,000 added since the previous business day is one unit. Remainders carry only across consecutive increases. Each unit spends 2 % of the burn pool. That is the Federal Reserve's 2 % inflation goal, pointed the other way (config/burnkeeper/deficit.rules.json). The devnet rehearsal coin used 1 %.

Some topics are refused whatever the source (config/burnticker/refuse.json, applied at create time). They are deaths, violence, terror, casualty counts, disasters and named private individuals. Names that claim to be official are refused. So is any selector on the coin's own trading, which means never the token's price. Elections are allowed.

3.4 Settlement coverage

The coverage audit (src/lib/burnticker/coverage/) checks every category in config/burnticker/categories.json and every selector kind the wizard can emit. For each one, it asks whether a coin can be settled without Kalshi. A category is settleable only when it has at least one primary source and an independent cross-read of the same fact. A primary source here means a first-party adapter that is attested-http or on-chain, tier 4 or better, launchable, and not an aggregator. A category is adjudicated-only when Polymarket's binary markets are its only route. Otherwise it is uncovered. Each source also gets a 90-day dry run. The dry run compares the readings its schedule expected with the readings it produced. It measures the median delay from the end of a period to the keeper's first sighting. It also measures how often the primary and its cross-read disagree.

On the frozen fixtures of 2026-10-10, five of eleven categories are settleable (docs/burnticker/SETTLEMENT-COVERAGE.md). The first is Sports. Each game settles on its Polymarket on-chain payout where a moneyline market exists. Otherwise it settles on TheSportsDB under its commercial license, confirmed by the league's own feed (MLB, NHL or NBA) or by API-Sports. Nothing is read from ESPN, and schedules come from the leagues' own feeds or from TheSportsDB. The second is Economics, where four series are each checked against a cross-read. They are fred.UNRATE against BLS LNS14000000, fred.CPIAUCSL against BLS CUSR0000SA0, bls.eggs against FRED APU0000708111, and treasury.debt against FRED GFDEBTN at quarter ends. There were 0 disagreements in every shared month. The third is Financial markets, where Pyth is checked against RedStone. The fourth is Weather & Climate, where the NWS climate report is checked against the Iowa Environmental Mesonet. The fifth is Crypto & On-chain, where bitcoin.blockHeight is checked against blockstream.info, and Pyth against RedStone or the pool TWAP. The other six categories are adjudicated-only, and none is uncovered. Reading a fact twice catches a broken endpoint, a parser error or a tampered response. When the cross-read is a mirror, as when FRED republishes BLS, it does not catch an error in the original release.

Creation first runs the selector live once (preflightSelector, which is wired into the launch pipeline). The primary and its cross-read are both read. Creation is refused on a Kalshi rule, a missing cross-read, a failed read, or a disagreement on the latest shared period. The coverage matrix is served at GET /api/burnticker/v1/coverage, drawn on the trust page and rendered to docs/burnticker/SETTLEMENT-COVERAGE.md. The test suite fails when any category is uncovered.

3.5 How burns are settled

A coin settled by a market is read every 5 minutes. A coin settled by a published data feed is read on that feed's own schedule, and a feed with no schedule of its own is read every 60 minutes. $DEFICIT reads the Treasury's debt figure at 4:30 p.m. and 8:00 p.m. New York time on business days. A result counts only when it is final: a game when its feed marks it final, a Polymarket market only after its on-chain payout is recorded, which follows UMA's dispute window of about 2 hours (longer if a proposal is disputed), and a scheduled publisher's number when it is posted. The cycle that sees a final result submits the reading and runs the burn in the same pass. Before it swaps, the program checks the pool's current rate against its average over the last 10 to 20 minutes, which a trading pool already has. One burn spends at most 2% of the pool's SOL reserve, and the rest carries to the next burn, so a large burn arrives in slices. Typical times after the event are about 5 to 25 minutes for sports results and scheduled data, and about 2 hours plus a few minutes for Polymarket markets. When the transaction confirms, the receipt appears on the coin's page. The page How burns are settled states the same facts, each number read from the code.

4.3 initialize_token

Three keys sign it: the payer, the creator and the new mint keypair. The mint does not need to exist yet. So only the launcher can create the config for a mint, and only once. The instruction records the rule, the feed id, the memo text, the rules hash, the attester set and the quorum. It funds the fee PDA with the reserve. It enforces the swap-safety bounds of section 4.5. For buyback policy 0, it also pins the key of LaunchLab's PlatformConfig account, its cpConfigId, and its LP lock and burn scales. It refuses a platform whose platformCpCreator is set to anything other than the fee PDA, or whose scales cover less than 50 %. It takes no token program or token account, because the program has no mint path.

4.4 Readings

Anyone can send submit_reading(record_date, value, source_url_hash, proof_hash), but it is valid only with a quorum of attester signatures. Each attester signs exactly these bytes (src/attest.rs):

BURNRECEIPTS-v3|<cluster genesis>|<program id>|<mint>|<feed_id>|prev=<previous date or none>|<YYYY-MM-DD>|<value>|src=<sha256(source URL), hex>|proof=<proof locator, hex>

The signatures travel in an Ed25519SigVerify instruction in the same transaction, with one shared message and one entry per key. The program reads them back through the instructions sysvar. It requires every offset to point into that instruction, counts each key once, and matches the message byte for byte. The message binds the genesis, the program id and the mint, so a signature is useless on another cluster, token or deployment. It also binds the previous date, which fixes the order, so pre-signed readings cannot be reordered or skipped. An attester set holds at most 5 keys, and the quorum is at most 3. That is the largest quorum that fits a legacy transaction, which is 966 bytes for 1 key, 1,076 bytes for 2 keys and 1,186 bytes for 3 keys.

Dates strictly increase, and no future date is accepted. Readings are never edited, deleted or closed. Units follow the rule (src/math.rs, mirrored in src/lib/burnReceipts/math.ts):

  • On an increase, units = floor((delta + carry_in) / unit_value). Under carry rule 0 the remainder carries across consecutive increases. Under rule 1 it is dropped.
  • A reading that goes down or stays flat earns nothing and resets the carry.
  • A quorum-signed reading whose change exceeds max_abs_delta is accepted as a RESET. It gives 0 units, resets the carry, sets a new baseline and emits ReadingReset. It never wedges the feed (review 1, M-2).

How event rules use the unit counter. The program's reading model is one value per UTC date. A Burnticker token is initialised with value_decimals 0, unit_value 1, no carry and the feed id bt.units:<TICKER>. The attested value is a counter, defined as value(D) = 1 + every unit the token's settled receipts carried through date D. The day's delta is the day's units, so one crank covers all of a day's settlements. The signed src hash commits to the exact events counted, as sha256("burnticker://<TICKER>/units/<YYYY-MM-DD>?events=<id,id,…>") (src/lib/burnticker/program.ts). The current program cannot express a rule that pays different fractions for different outcomes, and the launch bundle refuses such a rule.

4.5 crank_buyback: swap, burn and memo in one instruction

crank_buyback(record_date) processes the next reading in line, which is the one where prev_date == crank_cursor. The cranker supplies no amount, pool, rate or recipient. The steps run in this order (src/instructions/crank_buyback.rs):

  1. Pool. The pool is pool = fee PDA lamports − reserve + WSOL already held (spendable_pool). While the pre-audit cap is in force, the rule is applied to min(pool, cap). When the cap binds, BurnPoolCapBound is emitted, and the Reading keeps both the basis and the actual pool.
  2. Rule spend. The spend is spend = pool × (1 − (1 − f)^units), where f = fraction_per_unit_bps / 10,000. It is computed in u128 fixed point as keep = floor(pool × pow_floor(1 − f, units) / 1e18) and spend = pool − keep, so the spend never exceeds the pool. It agrees with the keeper's exact rational result to within one lamport for pools under 10^15 lamports, and a test pins this. The amount due is the rule spend plus any spend carried from earlier cranks.
  3. Spend floor. Under floor_lamports, nothing is bought. The reading is processed with the memo swap=none, and the pool keeps the SOL.
  4. Per-crank cap. One crank spends at most 2 % of the pool's WSOL reserve. The rest is carried (carried_spend_lamports, SpendCapBound). Small, frequent swaps limit trade impact and leave less room for a sandwich.
  5. TWAP guard. Initialisation enforces max_twap_deviation_bps ≤ 300 and a twap_window_secs of 600 to 1,200. The spot exchange rate (tokens per SOL) may not sit more than the deviation below the CPMM oracle's time-weighted average. The oracle records the rate before each swap, and only across distinct seconds, so a front-run in the same block cannot move it. A refused crank defers the burn day. The burn is late, not lost.
  6. Minimum output. min_out is the larger of two quotes. Each quote is the constant-product output, less the pool's whole fee and less slippage_bps (≤ 300). One quote uses the live reserves. The other uses a pool of the same SOL depth at the TWAP rate. A push inside the same transaction lowers the first quote but not the second. So a sandwich can take at most slippage_bps of a spend, and that spend is itself at most 2 % of the reserve.
  7. Swap. A system transfer moves SOL from the fee PDA to its WSOL account. Then a CPI calls Raydium CPMM swap_base_input on the registered, verified pool. The CPMM program id is hard-coded. The vaults, amm_config and the observation account are checked against the pool.
  8. Burn. Every token the fee PDA holds is burned. That is what the swap delivered, plus any token-side creator fees or donations.
  9. Memo. The program calls the Memo program through a CPI with the canonical v2 memo (section 13.4), built from the accounts alone.
  10. Events. The program emits ReadingProcessed through emit_cpi!, and also SpendCapBound, SpendCarried and BurnPoolCapBound when they apply.

crank_vault is the vault-mode twin. It computes owed = min(units × tokens_per_unit + shortfall_in, allocation_left) and burn = min(owed, vault). The rest becomes a public shortfall carried forward, and the memo is v1. crank_no_units marks a zero-unit reading as processed.

The measured cost is about 110,000 compute units in a typical case and 137,009 in the worst case. The crank transaction is 907 bytes.

4.6 How fees reach the burn pool

The fee PDA is LaunchLab's recorded creator. LaunchLab's initialize_v2 takes creator as a non-signer, so the launch passes feePda(programId, mint) there (docs/burnkeeper/fees.md section 1). A devnet simulation with a PDA creator succeeded and used 188,143 compute units.

  • On the curve. LaunchLab accrues the creator fee in its vault [fee PDA, WSOL], and only the creator's signature can claim it. Anyone can call claim_launchlab_creator_fee. It calls LaunchLab's claim_creator_fee with the fee PDA signing by seeds, and the recipient is pinned to the fee PDA's own WSOL account.
  • After graduation. When the platform's platformCpCreator is unset, the CPMM pool records the launch creator as pool_creator. Raydium's collect_creator_fee_permissionless pays that creator's token accounts (ATAs). Any payer may call it.
  • Seed deposits. Anyone can send SOL to the fee PDA. Nobody can take it out.

4.7 Pool registration and provenance

*Pair coins (registration policy 2, section 5.4) register through register_pair_pool instead. Everything below describes the LaunchLab path, which is unchanged.*

Anyone can call register_pool, once. It accepts only LaunchLab's own migration pool:

  1. The token's LaunchLab pool (["pool", mint, WSOL] under LaunchLab) records the fee PDA as creator, CPMM as its migration destination, and the platform pinned at init.
  2. The CPMM pool is the deterministic PDA ["pool", cpConfigId, token0, token1] of the cpConfigId pinned at init.
  3. Its pool_creator is the fee PDA.
  4. At least 50 % of its live LP supply is burned or held by Raydium's LP lock (ATA(LOCK_CPMM_AUTH, lp_mint)). This minimum sits on top of the provenance proof. To block registration, a griefer has to deposit more than the whole migrated liquidity and leave it there.

The mint must have no mint authority and no freeze authority. Burnticker's platform locks 100 % of graduation LP (section 6), so a correct migration passes with margin. One point is not confirmed yet. It is whether the real LaunchLab migration passes the creator through, creates the pool at the cpConfigId PDA, and locks or burns LP as its SDK says. LaunchLab is closed source, so this is a gate for the devnet rehearsal.

Stranding recovery. If no pool can be registered, or if the registered one stays untradeable for 7 days in a row, the token's admin may propose set_registration_policy to a fixed pool that passes the same creator and LP checks, plus either 90 % of LP burned or locked or the pool PDA of a LaunchLab-known AmmConfig. The proposal waits 48 hours and expires after 14 days. Any one current attester can veto it. The power ends at the pre-audit cap's sunset or at renounce_admin.

4.8 Post-CPI guards

Two instructions lend the fee PDA's signature to outside programs that can be upgraded: the LaunchLab claim and the CPMM swap. After each one, the program checks that the fee PDA is still System-owned, with no data and the expected lamports. It checks that each fee-PDA token account keeps its owner and mint, with no delegate and no close authority. It also checks that the swap's output vault paid exactly what the fee PDA received. Any violation reverts the transaction (FeeAccountTampered, SwapOutputMismatch; src/guards.rs). This is tested against a hostile stand-in loaded at LaunchLab's and CPMM's program ids (mocks/evil_callee).

4.9 Pool health and replacement

Anyone can call check_pool_health. It must be a top-level instruction, alone in its transaction with the pool. It records observations of the registered pool being untradeable, which means swaps are disabled, the account is gone, or the WSOL reserve is under 0.01 SOL. A spell starts on the second of two observations at least 1 hour apart. It stays continuous while observations are at most 24 hours apart. After 7 continuous days, anyone can call replace_pool to move to a different tradeable pool that passes every register_pool check. After 30 days, cranks process readings below the spend floor with the amount carried, so the queue never wedges. A Jito bundle of separate transactions is invisible to these checks, and that gap is accepted (pool_health.rs).

4.11 The pre-audit cap

While the cap is in force, each crank's basis is min(pool, 25 SOL). SOL above the cap stays in the fee PDA. Only the upgrade authority can call initialize_platform, and only once. It must set exactly the default, so the cap is never lowered. The cap can only be raised, through the 48-hour timelock, and it disappears at its sunset. It protects against program bugs. It does not protect against the operator, whose multisig can replace the code until the upgrade authority is finalised.

Every state change emits an event through emit_cpi!. Those events are inner-instruction data, which cannot be truncated the way logs can, so an indexer can rebuild state. There are 34 event types, listed in section 13.3. The one exception is PairResultSubmitted. It is a plain emit! log event, which appears as "Program data:" in the transaction's logs. submit_pair_result carries both coins' attester signatures and has no room for the self-CPI accounts. The PairResult account it writes is the durable record.

5. Rivalry pairs

Two coins launch together on the same tier-1 selector with inverted mappings (src/lib/burnticker/rules.ts invertMapping, launchBundle.ts buildPairBundles). For example, $BRONCOS burns on the NO of each -DEN leg of KXNFLGAME, and $CHIEFS burns on the YES. $BULLS and $BEARS burn on the direction of the S&P close (scalar up against down). A binary settlement burns exactly one side. A flat scalar settlement burns neither.

  • pair: { pairId, side, counterpartMint } is part of each side's canonical JSON, so the link is inside both locked rules. The two rules are hashed separately. Side A is created first. Side B's rule names A's mint as its counterpart, and A's counterpart is held in the registry's append-only pairs table.
  • Binary sides swap onYes and onNo. Scalar sides flip up and down. An abs rule cannot pair. Pairs must use a tier-1 adjudicated market source.
  • The scoreboard. The pair page shows both sides, the burn tally, the supply burned on each side, and the receipts of both coins in time order. For a binary pair, the burn counts of the two back-tests add up to the number of the selector's settlements that were not voids.

A relative-value perpetual version was considered and rejected. The reasons were pool-depth caps, oracle manipulation and the lack of a legal path in the US (LAUNCHPAD-PATH section 22).

5.1 Both or neither

Every pair created since 2026-10-10 carries a clause in both sides' canonical rules: pair.clause = "both-or-neither", with a voidBy date. That date is a one-shot's first settlement date, or else 30 days after launch.

  • Event burns on either side apply only once BOTH coins have a registered pool. A settlement read before that is recorded as a pending-pair receipt. In the cycle when the second pool registers, the pair goes live and the waiting receipts execute in settlement order.
  • If either side has no registered pool when the voidBy UTC date ends, the pair voids. Waiting receipts become voided, which is final. No event burn happens on either side again, and the rule has ended. Each coin's pool then winds down (section 5.3), once that coin has a pool, so no fee is stranded in a pool that can never burn.
  • The outcome, live or voided, is written once to the registry's append-only pair_outcomes table. The pair page shows the state, the void date and both bonding curves side by side.

5.2 Burn Derby: coin against coin

Burn Derby is a selector kind for pairs. It compares how two existing coins perform over a UTC day (00:00 to 00:00) or a week. The rule's derby block names both coins, the window, the measure and the season. For each coin it gives a Pyth feed id, a Solana mint, the deepest SOL pool and an independent cross-read. The measure is either the % change, or the change of circulating supply times the feed value. The rule's series is derived from the block, and the whole block is inside the rule hash (src/lib/burnticker/derby/spec.ts).

  • Settlement. A window's margin is coin A's % change minus coin B's, exact to six decimals. A positive margin settles YES, and side A's coin burns. A negative margin settles NO, and side B's coin burns. An exact tie is a void on both sides. The coin on the winning side burns, and nothing is paid to anyone. A one-shot variant settles once. It settles YES on the first window that ends with A's size above B's, or NO at the end of the season if that never happens.
  • Values. For Pyth, the values come from the sponsored PriceUpdateV2 accounts on Solana. Pyth's receiver program writes them after it verifies the Wormhole-signed update. They are read without a key and snapshotted at each boundary. The other option is the Hermes update published at the boundary, which is Pyth's pull service and needs an API key. The sha256 of its bytes becomes the proof. A coin without a Pyth feed is read from the time-weighted vault balances of its deepest SOL pool, priced with Pyth's SOL/USD feed.
  • Cross-read. An independent operator (Jupiter, DexScreener or RedStone) is snapshotted at the same boundaries. If its margin differs from the primary's by more than two percentage points, or if a value is missing, the window is withheld. There is no reading and no burn, and an alert goes out. Each window is decided once and stored append-only.
  • Proof on chain. The reading carries derby:sha256:<hex> of the window's proof document. The counter attestation's quorum-signed message carries the same hash as proof= (section 4.4). Its src= commits to the event ids, and those ids carry the first 16 hex digits of the hash.
  • Brackets. A bracket has eight or sixteen coins, with one derby per match per round. A round lasts seven days by default. The coin with more winning days advances. The tiebreak is the compounded change over the round, then the lower seed. The losing fan coin retires and reads nothing more. A winning day in the final counts as two units. The program compounds them as pool × (1 − (1 − f)²), while two times f stays within the rules' 10% maximum.
  • Refused. A derby refuses a Burnticker coin as an input, anything on the refuse list, the same coin twice, and a coin with no Pyth feed and under $250,000 of liquidity. It also refuses US index proxy feeds unless the host enables them. A coin's own trading is never an input.

5.3 No pool is left behind

Every rule created since 2026-10-10 carries onEnd: { windDownDays: 28, sweepThresholdLamports } inside its hash.

  • The wind-down. A rule can end for several reasons: a one-shot resolved NO or void, a pair voided, a derby season over, a bracket coin knocked out, or a team's season over. When it ends, the keeper attests a daily reading from a date-based clock whose proof is sha256("clock|<date>|<rules sha256>"). Each day counts the smallest number of units n of the rule's own fraction f for which (1 − f)^(28n) ≤ 1 %. At 1 % that is 17 units a day. So at least 99 % of the pool is spent within 28 days. The 2 % per-crank cap carries the rest forward, and the $1 floor ends the tail. Then the coin retires.
  • A one-shot at 100 %. A one-shot mapping may spend the whole pool. Recurring rules stay at 10 % or less. The program already accepts 10,000 basis points. A crank spends at most 2 % of the pool's reserve and needs a reading with units. So after the YES, the keeper attests one drain unit a day until the program carries nothing more. Each drain unit's rule spend is exactly the whole pool.
  • The sweep. A retired coin's burn pool keeps receiving the post-graduation creator fee. When its spendable balance passes the threshold, the keeper attests one full-spend reading, at most once a week. A permissionless instruction that lets anyone do this without the keeper is queued for the next program review (PROGRAM-UPGRADE-QUEUE.md). So is forwarding a losing coin's leftover to the winning coin of an outcome set.

One idea was to let communities add to a score by burning their own tokens into the program. It was considered and not built. It would turn the score into a function of spending, and it would add a deposit path to a program designed without one.

5.4 The pair curve: both coins graduate, or neither does

On LaunchLab each coin of a pair has its own curve, so one side can graduate while the other stalls. On 2026-10-10 the owner decided on a second program that runs both curves of a pair together (pair_curve, described in docs/burnticker/PAIR-CURVE.md). A pair launches through it when BURNTICKER_PAIR_CURVE_ENABLED is set. The setting is off by default, and single coins stay on LaunchLab. Until it is on, pairs launch on two LaunchLab curves under the rules-level "both or neither" clause of section 5.1.

  • One account per pair. Each pair has one account with two virtual-reserve bonding curves. By default they use pump.fun's constants: 30 SOL virtual, 1.073 B virtual tokens, 793.1 M sellable and 206.9 M reserved for the pool. There is one System-owned SOL vault. Each coin has 1,000,000,000 tokens at 6 decimals, with mint authority revoked at creation and no freeze authority. The curve is path-independent. After any sequence of trades the vault holds exactly F(sold) = ceil(V_s · sold / (V_t − sold)) per side, so selling everything back drains it exactly.
  • The fee is 1 % of every curve trade, which is 100 basis points. Of that, 25 basis points go to the developer wallet, which is the claim wallet of Burnticker's own LaunchLab platform, compiled into the program. The other 75 basis points go to the traded coin's fee PDA in the settlement program (burn_receipts). Both shares stay in the pair's vault until they are due. The 75 basis points reach the fee PDA when that coin's pool is created, or go back to its holders on a void. The 25 basis points are paid by a permissionless claim. No trade pays any other account, so no outside wallet can stop trading. There is no Raydium curve fee, no referral and no creator slice.
  • Graduation. Graduation needs the combined raise to reach the pair's threshold before the deadline. Each side must hold a minimum share of it, which is 25 % by default and at most 40 %. Both sides must also be able to pay for their pools. The trade that meets this commits the pair on the spot. Both curves close, and the pair can no longer void. Each graduate call then creates its side's CPMM pool at a pair_curve address that nobody else can sign for. It puts in SOL and tokens in the curve's closing ratio, and it burns every token the pool did not need. Then anyone can call lock_lp, which locks 100 % of the LP with Raydium's LP lock. The Fee Key is owned by the fee-keys PDA of burn_receipts, and the LP is locked forever (section 6). Graduation takes two transactions, because both pools in one transaction would sit at Solana's 64-entry instruction-trace cap, and a griefer could push them over it.
  • Void. If the deadline passes without graduation, buys close. Each coin's holders share what that side raised, pro rata. That is its curve SOL plus the 75 basis points held for its fee PDA. There is no fee and no advantage in leaving first. Once nothing circulates, the pair's accounts close. A side that Raydium still blocks three days after the commit is put into the same pro-rata exit on its own. So is any side still without its pool thirty days after the commit. Once the deadline has passed, nobody can leave along the curve ahead of the others.
  • Registration. The settlement program gained registration policy 2 and the instruction register_pair_pool. It accepts only the pool that the pair program recorded for that mint, at its pair_curve address, created by the pair's vault on the pinned cpConfigId, with ≥ 90 % of the creation LP burned or locked (section 4.7).
  • Powers. The pair program has no powers over funds. It has no admin, no parameter update and no withdraw. Its upgrade authority is the same Squads vault as the settlement program's, and it is to be finalised before the first public pair (docs/burn-receipts/pair-curve-powers-register.md). Two blind reviews on 2026-10-10 found five High issues in the first version. All of them are fixed and were re-run on devnet (docs/burn-receipts/CHANGELOG.md).

5.5 Winner takes the burn

The owner decided this on 2026-10-11 (LAUNCHPAD-PATH section 40): "the fees for both the winner and loser should burn the winner's supply." Every pair made since then carries pair.winnerTakesBurn: true in both sides' canonical rules. The flag is inside each hash. Pairs made before then keep their bytes.

  • Pairing on chain. Two LaunchLab coins pair by naming each other in initialize_token. Each one passes pair_mint, the partner's PairLink address, which is passed every time, and pair_void_by. That last value is the rules' locked voidBy, the same on both coins. Side A is provisional until side B names it back. If that does not happen within 7 days, both stay plain coins for good, and the keeper raises the pair-unmatched alert every day in the meantime. A pair_curve pair is linked by link_pair, which anyone can call right after init_pair, with the Pair account as the witness. register_pair_pool refuses until link_pair has run, or after an unlink. link_pair is refused only for a voided Pair or a refunding side (PairTerminal). Both coins must have the SAME attester set and quorum, with the same keys in the same order. This is checked at the second initialize_token and at link_pair (PairAttestersDiffer). Periods already read when the coins pair stay as unpaired history. Both coins record this in pair_after_date. No PairResult may name those periods, and the crank passes them as plain periods. So a coin that read first is never frozen. Pairing is deliberately left out of a coin's rules hash and params_hash. Those are fixed at initialize_token, while a pair_curve pairing happens later and can be undone. The record is the chain's pair_state (0 none, 1 provisional, 2 paired, 3 unlinked) and the PairLink accounts. A paired coin changes its attesters only through apply_paired_attester_change, with the partner's quorum co-signing the pending set. apply_attester_change refuses a paired coin (PairedAttesterChange). One risk remains, and it is stated plainly here. Whoever holds the common attester quorum decides every result. On Burnticker that is the operator's keeper alone, at quorum 1.
  • Each result. One PairResult account (["pair_result", mint_lo, mint_hi, date]) names the winner or a draw. The winner is the coin whose rule the result fires. No result may name a date before the pairing day, or a date on or before pair_after_date (PairPeriodUnpaired). Every result needs both coins' quorums. The two coins share one attester set, so one set of signatures covers both; a rotation applies to both coins at once (apply_paired_attester_change, program round 4). A win is recorded only when both coins have a reading for the date and the loser's reading is still unprocessed. Otherwise the call fails with PairResultNotServable. There is no pool condition. When the winner had nothing to read that day, the keeper first attests a flat 0-unit reading for it. A draw is posted only for a true draw and needs both quorums. The loser's crank_buyback for that period refuses (ForwardRequired). Anyone can then call forward_to_winner. It moves exactly what the loser's own rule would have spent, which is buyback_spend(min(spendable pool − the loser's carried spend, cap), units, f) with the loser's units and fraction. The amount moves from the loser's burn pool into the winner's. Nothing below the rule's minimum spend moves. A one-shot pair maps 100 %, so all of it moves. The instruction writes a memo in every case, in the form <LOSER PREFIX>|forward|from=<12>|to=<12>|period=<date>|lamports=N|v1. If either coin is not live (no verified pool of its own; a coin that burns another token is live before its own pool exists once the token it burns is pinned and still burning, read from that pin itself, round-5 review R5-9), or if the pair has been unlinked, it moves 0 lamports and still processes the loser's reading. So a dead side can never wedge the queue. Either way, a lost period never burns the losing coin unless no forward lands within 72 hours, in which case it burns as a plain period and a PairForwardMissing event is published. If the winner is dead, the fuel stays in the loser's pool, where the loser's own later burns spend it. The loser's reading is processed with outcome 6 (forwarded). The winner's ordinary cranks then spend its pool on the winner's coin and burn what they get.
  • What does not happen. The loser's supply is untouched, and nothing is paid to holders. SOL moves between two program PDAs that have no withdraw path. A draw or a void forwards nothing, and each coin burns its own fuel. If there is no result 72 hours after a reading, or if a LOST period's forward never landed within 72 hours, the coin cranks as unpaired (PairResultMissing or PairForwardMissing). That period can no longer forward. forward_to_winner is refused after the window (ForwardWindowClosed), so there is no race with the crank. At that point the planner switches from the forward to the paired buyback, and the keeper does not repeat the time-out. The keeper raises pair-result-missing 12 hours after the reading and pair-forward-pending 1 hour after it. It raises pair-result-unservable when a win cannot be recorded, and pair-forward-missing if a time-out happens. No draw is ever substituted for a result.
  • Unlinking. Anyone can call unlink_pair to turn a pair back into two plain coins, but only on TERMINAL on-chain evidence. For a pair_curve coin, the evidence is that the Pair is Voided or one of its sides is refunding. The Pair is required evidence for such a coin, and an Open pair is never unlinked, however long its curve runs. For a LaunchLab pair, the evidence is that the locked voidBy day has ended with a coin still without a verified pool. Otherwise the call fails with PairStillLive. Both coins become unlinked (pair_state 3), and a pair_curve coin still registers its pool. Both PairLink accounts close, and their rent goes to each coin's burn pool. Recorded results stay as history. The keeper sends the instruction when the evidence holds. The pair page then says "unpaired (reason)", and later receipts carry no pair role.
  • Order. The keeper attests both coins' readings, posts the PairResult, runs the loser's forward, and only then runs the winner's crank. That way the forwarded fuel is in the pool that the crank spends. After a one-shot pair's result, anything that reaches the loser's burn pool later, such as its end-of-rule burns and sweeps, forwards to the winner while the winner is live. On such a day the keeper attests a flat reading (0 units) for the winner, because a win needs both coins' readings for the date.
  • Receipts. The loser's receipt shows forwarded with the SOL moved. The receipt line reads forwarded from $LOSER: N SOL → $WINNER burn pool. The registry keeps an append-only pair_forwards table. The pair page tallies four numbers: burned on A, burned on B, forwarded from A to B, and forwarded from B to A. The Burn Derby scoreboard counts forwarded fuel. On devnet on 2026-10-11 (pair 6), one forward moved exactly 79,306,876 lamports from side B to side A, and side A's paired cranks then burned its coin (docs/burnkeeper/evidence/devnet-pair-forward-2026-10-11/).

8. Verification walk-through: recompute one receipt

Take the $DEFICIT reading for 2026-10-08. The program's own memo tests use it (src/memo.rs).

Step 1, the source.

curl -s 'https://api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov/services/api/fiscal_service/v2/accounting/od/debt_to_penny?fields=record_date,tot_pub_debt_out_amt&filter=record_date:eq:2026-10-08'

The row gives 40305316210829.72. The previous business day gave 40284036147367.19. The attested src field is the sha256 of that exact URL. Recompute it with printf %s '<url>' | shasum -a 256 and compare it with Reading.source_url_hash.

Step 2, the signature. Rebuild the canonical message of section 4.4 from the Reading and the TokenConfig. Then check each signature in the Ed25519 instruction of the submit_reading transaction against the attester keys listed in the TokenConfig.

Step 3, the units. The change is delta = 21,280,063,462.53 dollars. With carry 0 and a $1B unit, units = floor(21,280,063,462.53 / 1,000,000,000) = 21. The remainder 280,063,462.53 carries into the next reading if that reading is also an increase.

Step 4, the spend. Take f = 1 % and a pool basis of 1 SOL (1,000,000,000 lamports). The program's fixed-point arithmetic gives spend = 1,000,000,000 × (1 − 0.99^21) = 190,272,132 lamports, and buybackSpend in src/lib/burnReceipts/math.ts gives the same. Then apply the per-crank cap. If 2 % of the CPMM pool's WSOL reserve is smaller, the crank spends that, and the Reading's carried_out_lamports holds the rest. The memo's pool= is the basis actually used, so the arithmetic checks against the memo itself.

Step 5, the swap and the burn. The crank transaction is itself the swap and the burn. Its inner instructions hold three steps. The first is the CPMM swap_base_input from the fee PDA's WSOL account. The second is the SPL Token Burn from the fee PDA's token account, and supply falls by burn=. The third is the Memo CPI. Check that the swap's minimum output is at least the larger of the spot and TWAP quotes, less slippage_bps.

Step 6, the memo.

DEBT|2026-10-08|src=fiscaldata.treasury.gov debt_to_penny|prev=40284036147367.19|now=40305316210829.72|delta=21280063462.53|units=21|pool=<lamports>|spent=<lamports>|got=<tokens>|burn=<tokens>|swap=<first 12 chars of the CPMM pool>|rules=sha256:<12>|v2

swap= carries the first 12 base58 characters of the pool, because one instruction cannot know its own transaction signature. burn can exceed got when donated or fee tokens were also burned. memoFromChain() in src/lib/burnReceipts/ rebuilds the memo from the accounts alone.

For a Kalshi receipt, step 1 is the settled market. For example, curl -s 'https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?series_ticker=KXNFLGAME&status=settled' gives the market KXNFLGAME-26OCT04DENSF-DEN with status finalized and result no, settled at 2026-10-04T23:41:27Z. The saved fixture is config/burnticker/fixtures/kalshi/KXNFLGAME.settled.json. Step 3 applies the locked rule to each settlement of the day, and the counter's src hash commits to the event ids counted.

The public audit. The audit script (scripts/burnticker-audit.mts) recomputes every receipt of every token from the recorded readings and the published rule. It writes audit.json and exits non-zero on any mismatch. It reads no key and sends nothing. Its chain leg, which would compare each memo on chain, is not wired. The script runs with no program client and reports "chain not checked".

  • The plan is a Squads 2-of-3 vault with a 48-hour time lock. The owner holds two of its keys, and a trusted third party holds the third. The vault is the planned holder of the upgrade authority, the platform authority and each token's config authority. The program's own 48-hour timelocks come on top. The commands are in docs/burn-receipts/README.md section 5.2. The vault is not created yet.
  • Upgrade authority plan. Deploy the verifiable build, hand the authority to the Squads vault, and after review set it to none. That last step is the BPF Upgradeable Loader's SetAuthority with no new authority, executed as a Squads vault transaction. The runbook's launch gate says the mainnet launch is published only after solana program show prints Authority: none and the deployed hash equals the published verifiable hash (README section 5.3). Until then, every "no extraction" property depends on the multisig not shipping a malicious upgrade.
  • The LaunchLab platform admin is disclosed and has no timelock. Raydium's updatePlatformConfig has no way to change the admin, and the platform config PDA is seeded by the admin key. So the admin cannot be moved under Squads after creation (D-3). It can change the platform's fee rates, claim wallets, cpConfigId, platformCpCreator and LP scales for launches not yet migrated. Four things limit this. initialize_token pins what matters. verify-platform-pins re-reads the platform just before each create and refuses on any difference. The stranding switch covers a change between init and migration. The trust page shows the live config.
  • Mint keys prepared ahead of time (owner decision, 2026-10-14). Every coin made here gets an address that ends in burn. This site prepares those mint keys ahead of time, keeps them encrypted, hands each one to its creator once at launch and deletes it 24 hours later if that launch does not happen. Until the coin is created the key only decides the address; it cannot move anything. The creator's wallet still signs the launch, and the key is never used for anything but creating that one mint. When no prepared key is ready, the creator's browser finds one instead, and that key never leaves the browser. Other launchpads prepare addresses the same way.
  • Cranker allowlist during the cap. initialize_platform may name up to 3 keys that are allowed to call crank_buyback while the cap is in force. This guards against an outside cranker timing a multi-block TWAP hold. The list can only be cleared, by the platform authority or any listed key. It lapses 90 days after initialize_platform or at the cap sunset, whichever comes first. Readings, vault cranks, claims and health checks stay permissionless.

11. Limitations and risks

  • Settlement coverage. Without Kalshi, settled numbers exist only where a primary source has an independent cross-read. That is five of eleven categories today (section 3.4). Everything else settles on Polymarket's payout vector or not at all, and Polymarket is binary-only. Coins launched on a Kalshi rule before 2026-10-10 carry the event-contract disclosure on every page.
  • LaunchLab is closed source. The claim, the migration's pool address, pool_creator and LP handling rest on its SDK and docs. They have run only against a stand-in. Confirming them is a devnet-rehearsal gate, and it is not done.
  • Sandwich bound. A sandwich can take up to slippage_bps (≤ 3 %) of each crank's spend, and that spend is at most 2 % of the reserve. Once the allowlist lapses, a manipulator who holds the rate up for a full TWAP window can still shift a crank.
  • Quorum 1 at launch. The operator's key alone signs readings. A false reading is permanent and public, but it would size a burn.
  • Pre-audit cap. Each crank's basis is capped at 25 SOL until the sunset, so larger pools burn more slowly.
  • Upgradeable until finalised. The upgrade authority can replace every property in this paper until it is set to none.
  • Oracle voids and late data. A late source means no burn until it posts. A missed Treasury date cannot be inserted later, and the next delta absorbs it.
  • No adoption of existing tokens. initialize_token needs the new mint's signature, and the fee PDA must be LaunchLab's creator from the first trade.
  • Per-outcome fractions are not supported. The current program cannot express a rule that pays a different fraction for each outcome.
  • Burns wait on the curve. No buyback runs before graduation and pool registration.
  • Not built. Mainnet program deployment, the second attester, the audit's chain leg, and the systemd units for the multi-token keeper and the audit timer are not built.

For $DEFICIT the prefix is DEBT and the source is fiscaldata.treasury.gov debt_to_penny. For a Burnticker counter token the prefix is the ticker and the source is burnticker <selector>. In that case prev and now are counter values.
