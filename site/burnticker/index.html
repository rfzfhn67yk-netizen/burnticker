A Burnticker coin is a rule and its receipts. The rule says which settled result burns the coin and how much of its fee pool each burn spends. The receipt shows that it happened, in numbers anyone can check.

  1. The result settles on a primary source, and a second publisher on another domain reports the same fact.
  2. The reading is signed on chain by the attester quorum before any burn uses it.
  3. The program burns. burn_receipts spends the share the rule names from the coin's own fee pool, buys the coin on its own pool and burns every token it receives, in one instruction.
  4. The memo is the receipt: source, reading, units, pool, spent, got, burned, the swap and the rule hash. Each coin's page recomputes it from the source and shows ✓ only when chain and feed agree.

Nothing is ever won here — things are only burned. A coin pays nothing on any result. A season is a scoreboard of burns, one per league. Only when a league's season ends is the coin with the most receipts its champion: a stat on a scoreboard, never a recipient of anything.

Two layers

the engine

A launchpad with locked rules

One sentence becomes a rule: a settled result, a unit, and the share of the coin's fee pool each burn spends (1–10%). The rule is hashed at launch and written into the coin's on-chain config; a different rule is a different coin.

The rule is enforced by a program, not a promise. The fee pool has no withdraw instruction, and the program computes every burn from the rule and the signed reading: nobody chooses the amount.

the show

Seasons and rivalries

Team coins scored in receipts across the leagues, and rivalries that keep a running tally of burns. The scoreboards read the same receipts as the coin pages, so the show can never say more than the engine did.

No pot, no prize, no payout: a season ends with fewer coins in supply and a stack of receipts.

How a launch works

01

Describe the result

Type it — "Rays lose", "Lakers win", "Denver high above 90", "US debt rises $1B", "El-Sayed wins Michigan Senate" — or browse by category. Every suggestion names its source, who cross-reads it and when it settles; the back-test shows what the rule would have burned.

02

Set the dial, lock the rule

Choose what burns: a YES (or NO) result, or every full unit of a settled number ($1B of debt, one qualifying day), and the share of the fee pool each burn spends, 1–10%. Rules are locked at launch.

03

Launch

One wallet approval creates the coin on Raydium LaunchLab with its burn pool as the creator-fee recipient and registers the rule and its hash. You pay the venue's creation cost and gas; nothing to us up front.

Then burns happen, with receipts. When the result is in and its cross-read agrees, the reading is signed on chain, the burn is cranked — a permissionless instruction, except that a pre-audit list of up to three approved crankers can apply for at most 90 days, until the cap lifts — and the pool buys the coin at market and burns everything received in one transaction whose memo carries the result, the arithmetic and the rule hash. Nothing for a holder to run, nothing to withdraw.

The sources · primary data, cross-read

Every coin settles on a primary source with an independent cross-read of the same fact — another operator on another domain — or on the on-chain payout of an adjudicated Polymarket market. Prediction markets are for discovery; Burnticker shows their questions and rules, never a price.

Why you can trust a burn

Locked rules

The rule and its SHA-256 are written into the coin's config at launch and into every receipt. There is no instruction to change them. A different rule is a different coin.

Signed readings

Each reading is signed on chain as a Reading record before any burn uses it, by the attester quorum: one key at launch (the operator's data key); a second, independent attester is not built. Attesters are added under a 48-hour timelock, never removed below quorum.

The crank

crank_buyback computes the amount from the rule and the pool, swaps on the coin's own pool and burns in the same instruction; nobody chooses the amount. The instruction is permissionless, except that a pre-audit list of up to three approved crankers can apply for at most 90 days, until the cap lifts (on the Powers Register). Readings need the attester quorum — one key at launch, the operator's — so if the operator stops, readings stop and burns stop. Holders keep their coins, nothing can be withdrawn, every past receipt stays checkable, and a retired-coin sweep instruction is queued for the next program upgrade.

No withdraw instruction

The fee pool is a program-derived account. Trading fees flow in; anyone may deposit; the only way out is the burn. The program can still be upgraded; who holds the upgrade authority today, and under what time lock, is on the trust page.

Receipts anyone can recompute

One memo per burn, in a fixed order: date, source, previous and current reading, delta, units, pool, spent, got, burned, the swap signature, the rule hash. The trust page carries the sources, the coverage table, the reviews and the public receipts audit.

Holders are never touched

No permanent delegate, no transfer hook, no mint authority. Burns consume only what the pool bought. A coin that stops trading simply stops burning.

Who gets what

the coin

Its creator-fee share flows to its burn pool, which is the burn budget. Days with no trading are days with small burns. There is no team allocation and no team buy at launch.

the creator

Pays the venue's creation cost and gas, and earns nothing in cash from fees: the creator's slice is fixed at 0. What a creator gets is a coin whose burns are funded by its own trading. The review screen says this plainly.

burnticker

A platform fee of 25 bps of curve volume, set in the LaunchLab platform config and read live on the trust page. After graduation the LP is locked forever and its fees are split by code: 80% to the coin's burn pool, 20% to the operator. Nothing from burn pools, ever. The platform never buys any coin launched here.

Coins launched on Burnticker have no product, no revenue share and no promise beyond the published rule. Nothing is won on any result. Burning supply does not make a price go up; nobody here says it will. We refuse markets about deaths, violence, terror, casualty counts, disasters or named private individuals, and never the token's price as a source; we refuse names that impersonate people or institutions, and creators who use our pages to promise anyone anything.

Every number on every coin page is recomputed from the chain and the source at render time. If the page and the chain disagree, the chain is right. The program is reviewed, open source and not audited by a security firm. Not investment advice; nothing here is an offer.
