01

Pick the matchup on Kalshi

Search a settled Kalshi series and narrow it to the matchup — one team's game leg (KXNFLGAME, ticker contains -DEN), one daily close (KXINX). Pairs use tier-1 adjudicated markets only.

02

Name both sides, two signatures

Side A burns on one outcome; side B gets the inverted rule — the other outcome, or the other direction. Each rule is hashed and locked on its own with the same pair id in both. Two launch bundles, signed in sequence, A first.

03

The pair page is the scoreboard

Every settlement burns exactly one side. The pair page shows burns A : B, supply A : B, the last settlement with its market and result, the next scheduled market, and both receipts tables.

Same terms on both sides. One fee split for both coins, each with its own burn pool. LP burned at graduation. No creator cash from fees, no team allocation. A scalar move smaller than one unit burns neither side; a voided market burns neither.

How it works

01

Pick a Kalshi market

A recurring Kalshi series — every future market in it, narrowed by a ticker filter — or one specific market. The back-test lists every past market that matched and what the rule would have burned on each settlement.

02

Lock a rule

Choose what burns: a YES (or NO) settlement, or every full unit of a settled number (10 S&P points, 1 °F), and the fraction of the fee pool each spends, 1–10%. Rules are locked at launch. A new rule is a new coin.

03

Launch

One wallet approval creates the token on Raydium LaunchLab with its fee pool as the creator-fee recipient and registers the rule and its hash. You pay the venue's creation cost and gas; nothing to us up front.

Then burns happen, with receipts. When the market settles, the settled result is attested on chain, anyone can crank the burn, the pool buys the coin at market and burns everything received in one transaction — whose memo carries the result, the units, the pool, the spend, the swap and the rule hash. Nothing to run, nothing to trust, nothing to withdraw.

Or launch two at once: a rivalry pair. Same three steps, on a Kalshi market only: name two coins, and side B's rule is side A's inverted, so every settlement burns one of them. Two rules, two hashes, two signatures, one pair page that keeps score. See rivalries ↑

Admission rules. A settled Kalshi result, never the market's odds, never the token's price · unambiguous units · not controlled by anyone who can hold the coin · not a disaster. Elections and votes are allowed when an adjudicated market (a Kalshi settlement) is the source. Never: deaths, violence, terror, casualty counts, or named private individuals. Each rule reads the exchange's adjudicated result cross-checked against the series' own settlement source, and a voided market burns nothing.

Other sources. A few first-party feeds — U.S. Treasury's Debt to the Penny, NOAA solar flares, Launch Library orbital launches, JPL close approaches, BLS series, Wikimedia pageviews — stay available behind “Advanced: first-party feed” in the wizard, for things no market covers. $DEFICIT is the stated exception: it runs on Treasury's data directly, with Kalshi's monthly debt market as a cross-check.

Why you can trust a burn

Locked rules

The rule and its SHA-256 are written into the token's config at launch and into every receipt. There is no instruction to change them. A different rule is a different coin.

Attested readings

Each feed reading is signed on chain as a Reading record before any burn uses it. v1: our data key. v2: a second, independent attester per feed and a quorum of 2; attesters can be added under a 48-hour timelock, never removed below quorum.

Permissionless crank

crank_burn is callable by anyone. It computes the amount from the rule and the pool, swaps on the coin's own pool and burns in the same instruction. If we disappear, the public readings and the program keep working.

No withdraw instruction

The fee pool is a program-derived account. Trading fees flow in; anyone may deposit; the only way out is the burn. The program has no withdraw path, and the upgrade authority is a multisig, renounced once audited.

Receipts

One memo per burn, in a fixed order: date, source, previous and current reading, delta, units, pool, spent, got, burned, the swap signature, the rule hash. Each coin's page recomputes every burn from the source and shows ✓ only when chain and feed agree.

Holders are never touched

No permanent delegate, no transfer hook, no mint authority. Burns consume only what the pool bought. A coin that stops trading simply stops burning.

Who gets what

the coin

Its creator-fee share flows to its fee pool, which is the burn budget. Days with no trading are days with small burns. Optionally a disclosed slice (≤ 20%) of creator fees can go to the creator's wallet instead; default 0, shown on the coin's page.

the creator

Pays the venue's creation cost and gas. In the default template earns nothing in cash from fees — what they get is a coin whose burns are funded by its own trading. We say this plainly on the review screen.

burnticker

A platform fee on curve volume and the platform's share of post-graduation LP fees, program-enforced. Nothing from burn pools, ever. Our bots never trade coins launched here, and our own holdings of any coin are shown on its page.

Memecoins. The rule says what supply does, not what price does.

Coins launched on Burnticker have no product, no revenue share and no promise beyond the published rule. Burning supply does not make a price go up; nobody here says it will. We refuse markets about deaths, violence, terror, casualty counts, disasters or named private individuals, and never the token's price as a source; we refuse names that impersonate people or institutions, and creators who use our pages to promise anyone anything.

Every number on every coin page is recomputed from the chain and the source at render time. If the page and the chain disagree, the chain is right. Not investment advice; nothing here is an offer.
