A real receipt

Verified devnet

A real burn from the devnet rehearsal on October 6, 2026.

This rehearsal coin spent 1% of its burn pool for each $1 billion unit. The rule proposed for mainnet spends 2%.

Treasury's Debt to the Penny rose $23.92B from 2026-10-05 to 2026-10-06, which is 23 units. The rule asked for 0.104 SOL of the 0.506 SOL burn pool. One crank may spend at most 2% of the trading pool's SOL reserve, so 0.0201 SOL bought 4,010,874.87 $DEFICIT. Every token bought was burned in the same transaction, and the rest carried over to the next crank.

How launching works
  1. Describe the result, or browse by category. Each suggestion says where the result comes from, who checks it and when it settles.
  2. Choose what triggers a burn. It can be a yes-or-no result, or every full unit of a settled number. Then choose the share of the fee pool each burn spends.
  3. Approve once in your wallet. That creates the coin on Raydium LaunchLab and records the rule. You pay the venue's creation cost and the network fee. Creators earn nothing from trading fees.

$DEFICIT

Proposed mainnet rule, not launched

This coin burns when the U.S. national debt rises. Each full $1 billion added to the Treasury's Debt to the Penny counts as one unit. Each unit spends 2% of the burn pool to buy $DEFICIT at market, and everything bought is burned. The devnet rehearsal coin ran the same rule text at 1% per unit.

Historical replay
We replayed the rule over the real Treasury figures from January 2025 to October 8, 2026. It would have counted 235 burn days and 6,490 units. This is a look back, and it predicts nothing. The second publisher, FRED, reports this debt only once a quarter. It can confirm the quarter ends but not each daily reading.

Memecoins. The rule says what supply does, not what price does.

Coins launched on Burnticker have no product, no revenue share and no promise beyond the published rule. Nothing is won on any result. Burning supply does not make a price go up, and nobody here claims it does. We refuse markets about deaths, violence, terror, casualty counts, disasters or named private individuals. A source is never the token's price. We also refuse names that impersonate people or institutions.

If a page and the chain disagree, the chain is right. The program is open source. No security firm has audited it. This is not investment advice, and nothing here is an offer.
