How the first burn happens

Nothing burns until the curve fills and the debt rises. $DEFICIT first trades on its LaunchLab curve. The curve graduates once buyers have put in its raise, which this page reads from the coin's own pool after launch, and a seed gift to the burn pool does not count toward that. A memecoin with a receipt for every burn.

The rule. Each business day, the Treasury publishes the national debt. Every full $1B it rose since the previous reading counts as one unit. Each unit spends 2% of the token's own burn pool, buying $DEFICIT on its Raydium pool. Over a day, the share spent is 1 − 0.98units. The figure is total public debt outstanding, so a day's change is new borrowing minus debt repaid. The on-chain program burns everything bought in the same instruction, and the burn leaves a receipt on chain.

$DEFICIT's burns are not spread out this way. Its keeper runs one burn for each reading, up to the same 2% of the trading pool's SOL, and anything above that carries to its next run.

Why 2%: the number is a nod to the Federal Reserve's 2% inflation goal. That is only where the dial came from. The rule reads nothing from the Fed.

§5Verify it yourself

The rule file and its hash

The rule is fixed at launch and published as rules.json. Its SHA-256 hash is written into every burn's memo. A receipt that cites a different hash is not using this rule.

sha256: 8e3e8b66748a7630b9fe0e9793583434a076f2a03e335cad3d4c965758a15be9

To check it yourself, run curl -s https://deficit.burnticker.com/rules.json | shasum -a 256. This browser also hashes the served file again when the page loads.

Three steps you can run on any computer

  1. Fetch the two Treasury rows a burn used. The API is public and needs no key. 
    curl -s 'https://api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov/services/api/fiscal_service/v2/accounting/od/debt_to_penny?fields=record_date,tot_pub_debt_out_amt&filter=record_date:in:(PREV_DATE,RECORD_DATE)&sort=record_date'
  2. Apply the rule. 
    units = floor((now − prev + carry) / 1,000,000,000)
spend = pool × (1 − 0.98^units)        # pool = the burn pool's SOL − 0.02 reserve
burn  = every token the swap delivered
    Carry is the remainder under $1B from a run of rises in a row. Any flat or down day resets it to 0. This short recipe gives the planned spend. The program also applies an execution cap, the 0.02 SOL reserve, carried spend, a spend floor and rounding, so a burn can spend less than this or wait. The receipt checker runs every one of those checks.
  3. Read the memo on the burn transaction. It carries the same numbers, in this order. 
    DEBT|2026-10-08|src=fiscaldata.treasury.gov debt_to_penny|prev=40284036147367.19|now=40305316210829.72|delta=21280063462.53|units=21|pool=…|spent=…|got=…|burn=…|swap=<pool12>|rules=sha256:8e3e8b66748a|v2
    In the memo, swap is the first 12 characters of the Raydium pool address the program bought on, or none when nothing was bought. The transaction that carries the memo is itself the swap and the burn. If the memo, the chain and the Treasury rows agree, the receipt is good. The keeper recomputes every burn from its recorded readings. It marks a burn ✓ only when they match. Anything else shows ! with the reason.

§6Who pays for the burn

1. Trading fees

Every trade of $DEFICIT pays a creator fee on the launch venue. Eligible trading fees fund the coin's burn pool. On the curve, that is the creator fee. After graduation, it is Raydium's creator fee plus 80% of the SOL side of the locked pool's liquidity fees. In all, about 0.88% of every swap goes to the coin's burn pool and about 0.04% to the operator — 20% of the locked pool's liquidity fees. Raydium keeps about 0.08%.

2. The fee pool

Fees are claimed into the burn pool. The pool is an account of the on-chain program. No instruction pays it out to a person or to the operator: the program spends it only on buying $DEFICIT and burning what it bought, and it never sells. Any seed deposit is shown with its transaction. The program itself can still be upgraded through the operator's Squads vault, and the Burnticker trust page says who can approve an upgrade.

3. The swap

On a day with units, the program spends 1 − 0.98units of the pool buying $DEFICIT on the token's Raydium CPMM pool. If that amount is under the floor of about $1, the program buys nothing and the pool keeps the money.

4. The burn

Every token received is burned in the same instruction, with the memo. Holders' balances are never touched. There is no delegate, no transfer hook and no mint authority.

What $DEFICIT is

$DEFICIT has no product, no revenue share and no team promise beyond running the published rule. Burns draw on the accumulated budget. Trading adds funds; a burn can be delayed or skipped when the spendable amount is too small.
